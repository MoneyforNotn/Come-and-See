# Editorial workflow

The public collection preserves ideas readers can understand, revisit, and
investigate. For resource-based entries, the first task is to convey the source
faithfully. Research then clarifies its claims and limits.

## 1. Capture the source

- Record the exact URL, title, creator, speakers, publication date, and duration.
- Identify excerpts, reposts, and their parent recordings. Publication and
  recording dates are different fields; leave unknown dates unknown.
- Prefer available captions to a paid audio-transcription service. Keep raw
  captions and extraction notes outside the public checkout and reuse them.
- Record whether captions are automatic, their coverage, and any unchecked
  audio or visual material. Captions do not authenticate a displayed document.

## 2. Map the argument before researching

Read the supplied material in full. Make a private, timestamped map of its main
question, thesis, reasoning, examples, proposed mechanism, and conclusion.
Preserve qualifications, conditional premises, and changes in emphasis. Attribute
host questions and guest claims separately. Do not import promotional claims
from a description when they are absent from the supplied excerpt.

When combining sources, identify shared ideas and disagreements. Count repeated
interviews with one speaker as repeated testimony, not independent confirmation.
Identify where the repository itself draws a connection.

## 3. Draft the source account

Use the [source-article template](../templates/source-article.md).

"In brief" summarizes the source's most relevant information and ideas. It must
let a reader recover the main argument even if the reader skips the rest. Give
the speaker's account fair space and clear attribution. A summary of a claim is
not an endorsement of it.

Develop the source account before the contextual checks. Use timestamped links
and original paraphrase; avoid copying long passages. Follow applicable source
summary and quotation limits. Link to omitted detail rather than making an
article a substitute transcript.

## 4. Add research that serves the article

Check central, consequential claims first. Prefer original records and research,
while assessing their provenance and interests. Official statements, institutional
self-descriptions, accusations, and findings establish different things.

Keep each research detour tied to a claim the source actually makes. Move long
literature reviews or experimental-design proposals into a clearly labelled
appendix when they would displace the source account. Cite specific supporting
documents and passages when available; a searchable archive's existence does
not verify an allegation supposedly contained in it.

Distinguish the five [evidence categories](../README.md#how-to-read-it).
Keep editorial status separate from those categories. Unverified, disputed,
contradicted, and currently untestable are different assessments. Philosophical
questions can remain open; lack of disproof does not establish a factual claim.

For allegations, distinguish specific actors and conduct from identity, religious
membership, association, meetings, or shared surnames. Historical continuity and
a chain of command require evidence beyond thematic resemblance.

## 5. Review twice

**Source fidelity:** Does the opening cover the central thesis and its important
supporting steps? Are the speaker's qualifications and the host's questions
retained? Does supplementary research serve the subject rather than replace it?
Are contradictions between sources visible?

**Evidence and clarity:** Do citations support the exact wording? Are allegations,
inferences, beliefs, and findings distinguishable? Are uncertain audio, missing
documents, and limitations disclosed? Do links work? Is the article readable
without knowing the original source?

An article can pass an editorial check and remain a draft with unresolved facts.
Record material corrections and the date and scope of each review.

## 6. Prepare publication

Keep private notes and transcripts outside the checkout. Inspect the local diff
for unintended changes, preserve legacy material with its status, and update
navigation after renames. Commit and push when requested by the owner.

## What changed after the first two pilots

**October 25, 2026:** The first pilot's opening evaluated the hypothesis before
adequately explaining it. Its contextual research then dominated the article.
The revised process checks source fidelity separately and puts the attributed
summary first. The second pilot also tests two interviews with the same speaker,
unattributed legacy notes, and the difference between a narrative connection and
documented coordination.
