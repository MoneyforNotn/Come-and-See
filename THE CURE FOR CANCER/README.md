### **Do you really believe that the trillion dollar cancer industry is researching for a way to put themselves out of business?**

If cancer is truly a trillion-dollar industry, it raises an uncomfortable question.. would the system built around it actually support a permanent cure if it meant losing that revenue?   

Historically, much of modern healthcare has focused more on managing illness than eliminating it at the root -through ongoing treatments, prescriptions, and repeat interventions.   

There's far more profit in long-term sickness than in prevention. That's part of why approaches like nutrition, lifestyle changes, and immune system often get overlooked or dismissed-because they don't generate the same level of recurring income.  

There's also growing in discussion around how the immune system interacts with certain infections. Some research has explored how natural immune responses may play a role in reducing risks for certain diseases, and even how specific viruses are being studied for targeted therapies.  

But instead of fully exploring these relationships, the current model often leans towards standardized, one-size approaches that may overlook long-term immune balance.   

At the end of the day, it's worth asking: is the system designed to truly create health... or to manage disease?
